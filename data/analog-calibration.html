Guided light calibration

01 / KNOW YOUR CHANNELS

Five outputs. Five simple checks.

Each button lights just that emitter, bypassing color trims. Check that the actual strip shows the named color. If it does not, correct the wiring before balancing color.

Use the same room lighting and projector mode you normally watch with. Start at low test brightness.

02 / YOUR NEUTRAL REFERENCE

Start with white made from RGB.

Show a neutral gray or white reference on the projector. Turn on the RGB reference and compare the light reflected from the wall. Reduce the color that looks too strong; keep at least one trim at 100% when possible.

Too pink? Reduce red or blue. Too green? Reduce green. A phone or laptop screen is not a calibrated white reference. Visual matching improves consistency; a meter is needed to establish color accuracy.

03 / MATCH BEFORE YOU MIX

Make the white LEDs agree with RGB.

Switch between A and B at the same test brightness. Adjust B until its tint and brightness are as close to A as possible.

Cool LEDs onlyWarm LEDs only

Your strip’s nominal endpoints are about 2800 K and 6500 K. This is a blend control, not a measured Kelvin setting. If B is too dim even at 100%, reduce the three RGB trims together in step 2 and compare again.

If the white LEDs keep a green or pink tint that you cannot match, leave their playback share at 0% in the next step. Warm/cool blending cannot correct every tint.

04 / BRING IT TOGETHER

Choose the white share. Check the shadows.

Decide how much of the shared neutral part of a color the white LEDs supply. Pure red, green, and blue always stay on RGB. Start at 0%, then increase only if you matched the whites in step 3.

All RGBAll shared white on WW/CW

The ramp uses 16-bit levels, from black to 3% light and back over 8 seconds. Look for abrupt tint changes or uneven fades on the wall. Color buttons are input references, not simulations of your LEDs.

What the new calibration changes

RGB16 arrives from the AnimeJaNai addon with gamma 2.2. The controller decodes it once, mixes and trims in linear light, and drives 12-bit PWM with fractional duty. The fractional part provides finer average brightness; it is not a native 16-bit PWM timer. No on-screen preview can predict your strip’s spectrum or the color of your wall.

Saving activates this new engine. Until then, existing saved calibration remains in use. Network, firmware update, and standby settings are kept.
